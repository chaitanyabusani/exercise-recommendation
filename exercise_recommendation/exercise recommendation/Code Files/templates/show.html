Information Regarding BodyFat Estimation

Context

Lists estimates of the percentage of body fat determined by underwater
weighing and various body circumference measurements for 252 men.

Educational use of the dataset

This data set can be used to illustrate multiple regression techniques. Accurate measurement of body fat is inconvenient/costly and it is desirable to have easy methods of estimating body fat that are not inconvenient/costly.

Content

The variables listed below, from left to right, are:

  1. Density determined from underwater weighing
  2. Percent body fat from Siri's (1956) equation
  3. Age (years)
  4. Weight (lbs)
  5. Height (inches)
  6. Neck circumference (cm)
  7. Chest circumference (cm)
  8. Abdomen 2 circumference (cm)
  9. Hip circumference (cm)
  10. Thigh circumference (cm)
  11. Knee circumference (cm)
  12. Ankle circumference (cm)
  13. Biceps (extended) circumference (cm)
  14. Forearm circumference (cm)
  15. Wrist circumference (cm)

(Measurement standards are apparently those listed in Benhke and Wilmore (1974), pp. 45-48 where, for instance, the abdomen 2 circumference is measured "laterally, at the level of the iliac crests, and anteriorly, at the umbilicus".)

These data are used to produce the predictive equations for lean body weight given in the abstract "Generalized body composition prediction equation for men using simple measurement techniques", K.W. Penrose, A.G. Nelson, A.G. Fisher, FACSM, Human Performance Research Center, Brigham Young University, Provo, Utah 84602 as listed in Medicine and Science in Sports and Exercise, vol. 17, no. 2, April 1985, p. 189. (The predictive equation were obtained from the first 143 of the 252 cases that are listed below).

More details

A variety of popular health books suggest that the readers assess their health, at least in part, by estimating their percentage of body fat. In Bailey (1994), for instance, the reader can estimate body fat from tables using their age and various skin-fold measurements obtained by using a caliper. Other texts give predictive equations for body fat using body circumference measurements (e.g. abdominal circumference) and/or skin-fold measurements. See, for instance, Behnke and Wilmore (1974), pp. 66-67; Wilmore (1976), p. 247; or Katch and McArdle (1977), pp. 120-132).

The percentage of body fat for an individual can be estimated once body density has been determined. Folks (e.g. Siri (1956)) assume that the body consists
of two components - lean body tissue and fat tissue. Letting:

  • D = Body Density (gm/cm^3)
  • A = proportion of lean body tissue
  • B = proportion of fat tissue (A+B=1)
  • a = density of lean body tissue (gm/cm^3)
  • b = density of fat tissue (gm/cm^3)

we have:

D = 1/[(A/a) + (B/b)]

solving for B we find:

B = (1/D)*[ab/(a-b)] - [b/(a-b)].

Using the estimates a=1.10 gm/cm^3 and b=0.90 gm/cm^3 (see Katch and McArdle (1977), p. 111 or Wilmore (1976), p. 123) we come up with "Siri's equation":

Percentage of Body Fat (i.e. 100*B) = 495/D - 450.

Volume, and hence body density, can be accurately measured a variety of ways. The technique of underwater weighing "computes body volume as the difference between body weight measured in air and weight measured during water submersion. In other words, body volume is equal to the loss of weight in
water with the appropriate temperature correction for the water's density" (Katch and McArdle (1977), p. 113). Using this technique,

Body Density = WA/[(WA-WW)/c.f. - LV]

where:

  • WA = Weight in air (kg)
  • WW = Weight in water (kg)
  • c.f. = Water correction factor (=1 at 39.2 deg F as one-gram of water occupies exactly one cm^3 at this temperature, =.997 at 76-78 deg F)
  • LV = Residual Lung Volume (liters)

(Katch and McArdle (1977), p. 115). Other methods of determining body volume are given in Behnke and Wilmore (1974), p. 22 ff.

Exercise recommendations

Here are some examples of normal exercises that can help maintain overall fitness and body fat levels:
1. Walking:
Description: A low-impact exercise that can be done almost anywhere. Walking for 30-60 minutes a day at a brisk pace helps burn calories and improve cardiovascular health. Intensity: Low
2. Jogging/Running:
Description: A higher-intensity form of cardio compared to walking. Jogging or running for 20-30 minutes a few times a week helps increase stamina and burn fat. Intensity: Moderate to High
3. Cycling:
Description: Whether on a stationary bike or outdoors, cycling is a great way to improve leg strength and cardiovascular health. Intensity: Moderate
4. Swimming:
Description: A full-body workout that strengthens muscles, improves endurance, and burns calories without putting too much strain on joints. Intensity: Moderate to High
5. Yoga:
Description: Focuses on flexibility, strength, and relaxation. Yoga can help tone muscles and reduce stress, contributing to overall health. Intensity: Low to Moderate
6. Bodyweight Exercises:
Examples: Push-ups, squats, lunges, and planks. Description: These exercises help build strength and muscle tone without the need for any equipment. Intensity: Low to Moderate
7. Dancing:
Description: A fun way to burn calories and improve cardiovascular fitness. Dancing for 20-30 minutes a day can help maintain body fat levels. Intensity: Moderate
8. Stretching:
Description: Helps maintain flexibility and prevent injuries. Stretching exercises like reaching for your toes, side bends, and shoulder rolls are simple yet effective. Intensity: Low
9. Pilates:
Description: Focuses on core strength, flexibility, and body awareness. Pilates can help tone muscles and improve posture. Intensity: Low to Moderate
10. Light Weightlifting:
Examples: Using dumbbells for bicep curls, shoulder presses, and tricep extensions. Description: Helps maintain muscle mass and strength. Lifting light weights with higher repetitions is effective for toning muscles without bulking up. Intensity: Moderate
These exercises are suitable for most people looking to maintain a healthy level of body fat and overall fitness. They can be easily incorporated into daily routines and adjusted based on individual fitness levels and preferences.



Fat reducinge exercises

Fat-reducing exercises typically focus on cardiovascular activities, high-intensity interval training (HIIT), and strength training to increase metabolism and burn calories effectively. Here are some examples:
1. High-Intensity Interval Training (HIIT):
Description: Alternates between short bursts of intense exercise and rest or low-intensity periods. HIIT is one of the most efficient ways to burn fat. Examples: Sprinting for 30 seconds followed by 1 minute of walking, or performing exercises like burpees, jumping jacks, or mountain climbers in intervals. Intensity: High
2. Running/Incline Running:
Description: Running at a steady pace or on an incline (either on a treadmill or outdoors) increases calorie burn and helps reduce fat. Intensity: Moderate to High
3. Cycling:
Description: Cycling, especially at a higher intensity or on varied terrain, is excellent for burning calories and reducing body fat. Examples: Stationary cycling classes like spinning or outdoor cycling with varied resistance. Intensity: Moderate to High
4. Jump Rope:
Description: A high-intensity, full-body workout that improves cardiovascular fitness and burns a significant amount of calories in a short time. Intensity: High
5. Swimming: Description: Provides a full-body workout and is highly effective at burning fat due to the resistance water offers. Intensity: Moderate to High
6. Strength Training: Description: Building muscle helps increase resting metabolism, which means you'll burn more calories even at rest. Strength training combined with cardio is highly effective for fat loss. Examples: Weightlifting exercises like deadlifts, squats, bench presses, and kettlebell swings. Intensity: Moderate to High
7. Rowing:
Description: A full-body exercise that targets multiple muscle groups and burns a lot of calories. Rowing increases cardiovascular endurance and helps reduce fat. Intensity: Moderate to High
8. Circuit Training:
Description: Combines different exercises in a circuit format with minimal rest in between. This method keeps the heart rate up and maximizes calorie burn. Examples: A circuit might include push-ups, squats, burpees, and jumping jacks performed one after the other. Intensity: High
9. Kickboxing:
Description: Combines cardio and strength training with fast-paced punches and kicks. It’s effective for fat loss and toning the body. Intensity: High
10. Climbing Stairs or Stairmaster:
Description: An excellent lower body workout that significantly boosts calorie burn and improves cardiovascular health. Intensity: Moderate to High
11. Burpees:
Description: A full-body exercise that combines strength training with cardio, making it very effective for burning fat. Intensity: High
12. Spinning:
Description: A high-intensity cycling workout often done in classes with varying resistance and speed to maximize calorie burn. Intensity: High
13. Battle Ropes:
Description: A dynamic exercise that works your arms, shoulders, and core, and is highly effective for burning fat when done in short, intense bursts. Intensity: High
These exercises can be incorporated into a workout routine to target fat loss effectively. Combining these exercises with a balanced diet and proper rest will yield the best results for reducing body fat.
